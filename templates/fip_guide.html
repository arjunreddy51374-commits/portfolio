FIP USER GUIDE · RELEASE 1

From financial data to evidence-backed intelligence.

Use FIP to investigate financial performance, review reported figures, and follow calculations back to their sources.

Open the FIP platform →
Know what this release can do

FIP supports Manual, Automatic, and Hybrid ingestion jobs. Automatic retrieval is limited to reviewer-approved public CSV/JSON connectors; every extracted fact remains subject to validation and human approval. Dedicated company or government explorer screens and comparison dashboards are not included yet.

01 / GET STARTED

Your first analysis in six steps

  1. Choose a starting point and ingestion mode

    Open the published financial facts to review approved data, or use the controlled workspace if you have authorized staff tokens. New jobs default to Manual. Choose Automatic for a reviewed CSV/JSON connector, or Both / Hybrid to automate retrieval and validation while allowing corrections for exceptions. Save the mode, then queue a job separately; changing a setting does not start ingestion.

  2. Select an entity and reporting period

    Use the entity, period, and basis shown with each fact. Distinguish consolidated statements from standalone statements. For example, a study of Fortis Healthcare for FY2024–25 must state which statement basis it uses.

  3. Inspect the reported figure

    Check the source publisher, reporting period, original value, currency, unit, evidence reference, and validation status. Open the source document or official dataset through its recorded source URL when available.

  4. Review the calculation

    Choose a supported metric and check its definition, required inputs, formula version, periods, and units. FIP preserves the reported figures and the calculation lineage used to derive a result.

    Illustrative revenue growthPrior-year revenue: ₹800 crore · Current-year revenue: ₹1,000 crore((₹1,000 crore − ₹800 crore) ÷ ₹800 crore) × 100 = 25%Example only; this is not a reported result for Fortis Healthcare or any other company.

  5. Investigate the result

    Ask what changed, which figures support the result, whether periods are comparable, and whether source or validation warnings remain. Treat interpretations that need more evidence as open questions, not findings.

  6. Review, compare, and export

    Confirm the record is approved and published before treating it as part of the public dataset. Use the platform’s Excel, CSV, or Power BI JSON exports when available. Preserve the metric definition, reporting period, and lineage when sharing a result.

02 / COMPANY FINANCIALS

Corporate financial analysis

Question: Is a company’s financial performance improving?

  1. Find the company’s approved published facts.
  2. Choose the reporting year and statement basis.
  3. Review revenue, profit, assets, liabilities, and cash flow.
  4. Inspect available growth, margin, liquidity, and cash-flow calculations.
  5. Open each fact’s evidence and note warnings across periods.
Interpret carefully

Higher reported profit alongside lower operating cash flow may warrant a closer look at receivables, working capital, and non-cash items. It does not by itself prove financial distress or manipulation.

Outcome: A structured view of traceable figures, reproducible calculations, and questions for further research.

03 / PUBLIC FINANCE

Government budget analysis

Question: How do public receipts and spending compare?

  1. Find approved facts for the government or jurisdiction.
  2. Check fiscal year, budget stage, and institutional scope.
  3. Review revenue receipts, expenditure, capital spending, and fiscal indicators.
  4. Compare estimates with actuals only when definitions and coverage match.
  5. Verify the figures against the official budget or fiscal report.
Illustrative revenue deficitRevenue expenditure: ₹460 crore · Revenue receipts: ₹400 crore₹460 crore − ₹400 crore = ₹60 croreExample only. Follow the applicable reporting framework for official definitions and adjustments.

Outcome: A transparent analysis of fiscal figures, budget execution, and reporting limits.

04 / COMPARABILITY

Compare two companies

Compare revenue growth, operating margin, liquidity, debt burden, operating cash flow, and working-capital efficiency when the required published inputs are available.

Before comparing

Check that both companies use compatible reporting periods, accounting definitions, currencies, units, and statement bases. A higher margin alone does not make a company a better investment; valuation, business risk, and sector conditions also matter.

Outcome: A comparable scorecard with source evidence and methodology visible. This release does not provide a dedicated comparison dashboard, so comparisons may need to be assembled from the available facts and exports.

05 / FOLLOW THE EVIDENCE

Investigate a financial warning

  1. Open the flagged calculation or record in the review workspace.
  2. Check the relevant figures, periods, and source statements.
  3. Look for disclosed explanations such as borrowing, acquisitions, or lease liabilities.
  4. Review related cash flow and interest coverage where available.
  5. Record the evidence and any unresolved questions.

Outcome: An evidence-linked investigation signal, not an unsupported declaration that a company is unsafe or in distress.

06 / RECORD LIFECYCLE

Understand data and calculation statuses

StatusMeaning
ApprovedThe applicable review requirements passed. Approval is required before eligible records can be published.
PublishedAn authorized reviewer published the record. Approved release snapshots expose eligible published facts.
Needs ReviewAn identity, mapping, or other review issue needs investigation before the record can proceed.
Validation FailedOne or more required checks failed. Do not treat the figure as a valid published result.
SupersededA newer approved version replaces the earlier record; the earlier version remains in its history.
Missing inputA required figure is unavailable. A calculation that depends on it cannot be completed.
Unit or period mismatchThe inputs are not comparable as provided. Check their units, dates, reporting periods, and basis.
Reconciliation failedA financial integrity check did not reconcile. Investigate the source figures and definitions.

Some calculation issues are shown as validation or review details rather than as standalone lifecycle statuses. Read the record’s checks and warnings for the exact result.

07 / SOURCE LINEAGE

Where does each number come from?

For every reported fact or derived result, check:

  • Who published the information?
  • Which document or dataset contains the figure?
  • Which page, table, field, or record supports it?
  • Which reporting period, currency, and unit apply?
  • Which validation checks were performed?
  • Which formula and version produced a calculated result?
  • Has the record been reviewed and published?

A published result should trace back to its evidence, and its calculation should be reproducible.

08 / USE WITH CARE

Important limitations

FIP supports financial research and analysis. It does not guarantee investment returns or replace professional financial, legal, or accounting advice.

Public-source data may be incomplete, delayed, revised, or subject to reuse restrictions. Not every metric is available for every entity or jurisdiction. Automatic retrieval supports configured CSV and JSON sources only; it does not extract figures from PDF reports or independently verify a source document’s meaning. Scheduled jobs require the separate FIP scheduler worker to run against the same database.

Keep forecasts, interpretations, and investment recommendations distinct from reported facts and calculated historical metrics.
